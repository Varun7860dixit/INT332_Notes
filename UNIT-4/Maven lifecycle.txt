3. The Maven Lifecycle (The Golden Chain)
When you tell Maven to do something, it executes a sequence of phases. Crucial Exam Rule: If you call a phase, Maven will automatically execute every single phase that comes before it in order.

Here is the chain you need to memorize:

validate: "Hey Maven, check if my project folder structure is correct and if I'm missing anything obvious."

compile: Translates your human-readable .java files into machine-readable .class bytecode. (Drops them into a newly created target/ folder).

test: Runs your unit tests (JUnit). Pro-tip: If a test fails here, Maven will stop the whole build right on the spot to protect production.

package: Takes all those compiled .class files and zips them up into a neat little package, usually a .jar (Java Archive) or .war (Web Archive).

verify: Runs heavy integration tests to make sure the package actually works.

install: Takes your local .jar file and pushes it into your local computer's hidden Maven warehouse (usually found at ~/.m2/repository). Now your other local projects can use it.

deploy: Pushes the final version up to a remote company server (like Nexus or Artifactory) so your whole team can download it.
